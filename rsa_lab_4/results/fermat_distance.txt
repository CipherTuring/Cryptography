==============================================================================
Exercise 7: the distance between p and q, and Fermat's cost
==============================================================================

Experimental conditions
-----------------------
  Processor        Intel(R) Core(TM) i5-8300H CPU @ 2.30GHz
  Interpreter      CPython 3.12.10
  Repetitions      3 per point, average reported
  Moduli           48 bits, seed 4207, same p throughout

The two moduli the exercise asks for
------------------------------------
| Case | n | bits | distance | Fermat iterations | Fermat time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p and q close | 106486033108813 | 47 | 12 | 1 | 9.8 us |
| p and q far apart | 209678084385617 | 48 | 10,000,016 | 838,944 | 900.80 ms |

  The close pair is settled in 1 iteration: its factors are near
  enough that the first candidate, a = ceil(sqrt(n)), already works.
  One is the floor, so these two points alone cannot measure how the
  cost grows - only that it does. The sweep below is what measures it.

What the sweep shows
--------------------
  Fitted exponent      1.94   (the algebra predicts 2.00)
  Ten times the gap    30,002 -> 300,008 multiplies the work by 98, not by 10
  Rho, the control     1,416 to 2,945 iterations, with no trend in the distance
  Fermat is not a slow algorithm or a fast one. It is the right
  algorithm for a modulus whose factors were chosen too close
  together, and the wrong one for every other modulus - which is
  why key generation is required to keep p and q far apart.
